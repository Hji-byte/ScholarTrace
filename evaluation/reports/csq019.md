# Literature Review

What methods are used for training deep learning models with differential privacy, and how do they manage the privacy-utility-computation trade-off?

## Summary
Differentially private (DP) deep learning primarily relies on perturbing gradients or model parameters to protect sensitive training data, with DP-SGD serving as the foundational algorithm [2, 4]. However, standard DP methods often incur significant utility degradation and computational overhead [2, 3]. Recent advancements address these challenges through gradient encoding, alternative noise distributions, forward-learning techniques, and architecture-specific designs [2, 3, 5]. Managing the privacy-utility-computation trade-off involves complex hyperparameter tuning, Bayesian optimization, and efficiency optimizations like binary neural networks and secure multi-party protocols [1, 6, 8], though challenges remain in scalability and bounding arbitrary noise distributions [2].

## Core Mechanisms and Algorithms for Differentially Private Training
Differential privacy for deep learning can be classified into four main directions: input perturbation, output perturbation, objective perturbation, and utility optimization [4]. The primary algorithm, DP-SGD, protects training data by clipping gradients to limit sensitivity and adding calibrated Gaussian noise to the average gradients of random data subsets [2, 4]. The momentum accountant technique allows DP-SGD to compute much tighter privacy bounds, enabling high model prediction accuracies using single-digit privacy budgets [2]. Furthermore, differential privacy guarantees hold under various forms of composition; for instance, if sensitive training data contains multiple examples from the same person, an ε-differentially-private training results in an akε-differentially-private guarantee for each person, assuming at most k records per person [5].

Beyond standard DP-SGD, alternative techniques have been developed to enhance training. Gradient encoding maps gradients to a smaller vector space, followed by aggregation, noise addition, and error correction (denoising) [2]. Noisy cyclic mini-batch gradient descent is considered more suitable for practical training than DP-SGD variants that use random subsampling at each iteration [7]. Additionally, stochastic approximation of a dual formulation of the ReLU minimization problem enables the NoisyCGD method, which achieves privacy-utility trade-offs comparable to DP-SGD applied to two-layer ReLU networks [7]. Forward-learning algorithms inherently add noise during the forward pass and utilize randomness to estimate gradients, providing internal randomness protection to model parameters [3]. Finally, designing model architectures explicitly for privacy-preserving training from the start yields better privacy-utility trade-offs than applying DP optimizers to existing non-private architectures [5].

## Managing the Privacy-Utility Trade-off
Standard DP training solutions, particularly DP-SGD, typically result in large degradations in the prediction accuracy of trained deep neural networks [2]. To mitigate this, gradient encoding and denoising improve model utility without degrading DP guarantees, outperforming state-of-the-art DP-SGD by achieving tighter privacy bounds for the same accuracy [2]. Alternative noise distributions, such as the Student-t distribution, can also improve utility for the same privacy budgets, although deriving privacy bounds for arbitrary distributions remains non-trivial [2]. Integrating model-knowledge guidance into the training process further enhances privacy-utility trade-offs by bridging pre-existing knowledge and DP [4].

In multi-party and federated settings, balancing the trade-off is complex. Local differential privacy eliminates the need for a trusted server but results in accuracy losses one or more orders of magnitude larger than central differential privacy [6]. Conversely, adopting binary neural networks and introducing discrete noise in federated learning achieves client-level DP protection while maintaining performance gains [8].

Tuning hyperparameters to achieve optimal trade-offs is arduous. Private stochastic optimization methods can achieve the same privacy level through different hyperparameter combinations, such as increasing noise variance while reducing the clipping norm, but it is generally unclear which combinations yield the best utility [1]. This complexity arises because privacy is not always independent of hyperparameters, preventing a trivial reduction to non-private hyperparameter optimization [1]. Bayesian optimization can be used to empirically characterize and tune these hyperparameters in a computationally efficient manner [1].

## Computational Overhead and Efficiency Optimizations
While some adaptations improve the utility-privacy trade-off in DP-SGD—such as over-parameterized models, mega-batch training, averaging per-sample gradients across multiple augmentations, and temporal parameter averaging—they heavily increase computational costs [3]. Similarly, existing forward-learning methods like Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA) add noise to high-dimensional parameters, substantially increasing computational costs and limiting scalability for training complex models from scratch [3].

To address these overheads, the DP-ULR method operates directly on logits, reducing computational overhead compared to traditional zeroth-order methods. It introduces a batch sampling operation with rejection and a dynamically managed privacy controller, adding negligible privacy cost in large-scale applications while maintaining high parallelizability and suitability for non-differentiable modules [3].

In secure multi-party learning, balancing privacy, efficiency, and accuracy is challenging due to DP accuracy loss and high communication overhead. Frameworks like PEA utilize a secure DP-SGD protocol to mitigate these issues. Systems like TF-Encrypted can balance this three-way trade-off, requiring significantly less training time than alternatives like CryptGPU to achieve the same accuracy [6]. Furthermore, adopting binary neural networks in federated learning improves communication efficiency by uploading binary model parameters [8].

## Limitations and Open Problems
Deep learning models easily memorize sensitive, personal, or private data, making differential privacy a significant criterion for safeguarding privacy [3]. Despite advancements, standard DP-SGD still results in large degradations in prediction accuracy [2]. Deriving privacy bounds for arbitrary noise distributions remains a non-trivial challenge [2]. Furthermore, choosing utility-maximizing hyperparameters is complex because privacy is not always independent of the hyperparameters [1]. In multi-party settings, local differential privacy suffers from accuracy losses that are one or more orders of magnitude larger than those caused by central differential privacy [6]. Finally, while forward-learning methods like DP-ULR reduce overhead, scaling these techniques to highly complex deep learning architectures from scratch without incurring prohibitive computational costs remains an ongoing challenge.
