# Literature Review

What security and privacy attacks against federated learning have been demonstrated, and which proposed defenses remain limited under adaptive attackers?

## Summary
Federated learning (FL) systems face significant security and privacy threats, including gradient inversion [5], poisoning [8], and backdoor attacks [6]. While various defense mechanisms such as similarity-based filtering, clustering, and secure aggregation have been proposed, they often fail against adaptive attackers [2, 7]. Adaptive adversaries can bypass these defenses through coordinate-wise optimization, multi-objective adaptation, and exploiting non-IID data distributions [3, 6]. Current defenses remain limited by their reliance on independent and identically distributed (IID) data assumptions, single-metric evaluations, and lack of strategic adaptation, highlighting the need for more robust, combined, and game-theoretic defense strategies [1, 4].

## Demonstrated Security and Privacy Attacks
Federated learning environments are vulnerable to a range of security and privacy attacks. Gradient inversion attacks pose a severe threat to data privacy by recovering training samples directly from model gradients [5]. In the context of privacy-preserving protocols, adaptive poisoning attacks can disrupt model convergence in Local Differential Privacy (LDP) FL by injecting poisoned parameters crafted via reverse training to maximize global loss [8]. Furthermore, secure aggregation, which typically relies on statistical approaches to filter poisoned local models, has been shown to be insecure against category inference attacks [7]. 

Beyond privacy breaches, FL integrity is frequently compromised by backdoor and poisoning attacks. Adaptive backdoor attacks have demonstrated the ability to break existing defenses using as few as a single malicious client out of twenty [6]. Additionally, Reconnecting Malicious Clients exploit the open connectivity of FL systems by disconnecting and reconnecting with modified attack strategies to evade detection [7].

## Proposed Defense Mechanisms
To mitigate these threats, researchers have proposed several advanced defense mechanisms. The meta-Stackelberg defense addresses adaptive and mixed poisoning attacks by formulating adversarial FL as a Bayesian Stackelberg Markov game, integrating meta-learning and Stackelberg reasoning to enable strategic adaptation during online execution [1]. Another proposed defense, MESAS, analyzes all layers and the model independently using statistical tests to create a complex multi-objective optimization problem for adaptive attackers, aiming to remain robust against strong adversaries and inter-client non-IID data [3]. 

To counter identity-based evasion tactics, Identity-Based Identification (IBI) has been proposed to authenticate FL clients using cryptographic identities, mitigating threats from Reconnecting Malicious Clients [7]. Furthermore, research indicates that while single-typed defenses are vulnerable, combined defense variants such as HACSFT_Flame, HACSFT_Krum, and HACSFT_Multi-Metrics can remain undefeated against adaptive adversaries [6].

## Adaptive Evasion Strategies and Defense Limitations
Adaptive attackers employ sophisticated strategies to bypass or degrade the effectiveness of proposed FL defenses. Against similarity-based defenses like cosine similarity filtering, FLTrust, and FedAvgCKA, attackers use coordinate-wise optimization to craft updates that maximize similarity metrics while maintaining backdoor effectiveness [2]. Similarly, clustering-based defenses exhibit structural fragility and fail when attackers carefully craft updates to position malicious representations within benign clusters to evade detection [2]. Adaptive adversaries also defeat state-of-the-art defenses by leveraging client disconnect and reconnect processes, utilizing defense metrics to adjust attack parameters, or adapting through multi-objective optimization [6]. In scenarios involving update norm clipping, attackers bypass the defense by directly scaling malicious updates to exploit the predefined threshold [6]. More broadly, adaptive adversaries can circumvent defenses by increasing the poisoning data ratio, fixing model parameters, constraining the learning process, or scaling model updates [3]. In privacy contexts, simple adaptive attacks utilizing auxiliary data can overcome heuristic defenses like gradient compression to compromise privacy in vision and language tasks [5]. Furthermore, adaptive poisoning attacks can evade robust aggregation defenses in LDP FL by integrating the constraints of the robust aggregation directly into the adversarial optimization process to generate undetectable malicious updates [8].

Despite these proposed defenses, current mechanisms exhibit significant limitations when facing adaptive attackers. Existing defenses are typically non-adaptive and tailored to specific attack types, rendering them inadequate against advanced adaptive attacks and mixed attack strategies [1]. Simply combining existing defenses with manual tuning is also ineffective against mixed attacks due to interference between the defense mechanisms [1]. A major failure mode stems from data distribution assumptions; many existing defenses assume independently and identically distributed (IID) training data across clients, causing them to fail in non-IID scenarios [4]. Indeed, nine evaluated defense solutions have been shown to fail to maintain robustness in realistic scenarios featuring inter-client non-IID data [3]. Additionally, a full-information adaptive adversary that knows the aggregation algorithm, parameters, and benign updates can break 13 existing defenses using at most four malicious clients [6]. A strong adaptive adversary that balances multiple adaptation objectives and applies manual invasions on model parameters can similarly circumvent nine existing state-of-the-art defense methods [3]. Even advanced defenses have limitations; for instance, the MESAS defense does not leverage the benign updates of other clients, which is a critical vulnerability compared to full-information adaptive attacks that exploit such knowledge [6]. Finally, single-typed backdoor defenses contain a fundamental exploitable gap against adaptive adversaries, underscoring the necessity of combined defense strategies [6].

## Limitations and Open Problems
The available evidence highlights that while adaptive attacks consistently bypass single-metric and non-adaptive defenses, the development of universally robust defenses remains an open problem. Current defenses struggle with inter-client non-IID data and mixed attack strategies, indicating a need for defenses that do not rely on strict data distribution assumptions. Furthermore, the vulnerability of defenses to full-information adaptive adversaries suggests that future research must focus on mechanisms that do not leak benign update information or aggregation parameters. Developing combined, game-theoretic, and meta-learning-based defenses that can dynamically adapt without suffering from mechanism interference represents a critical direction for securing federated learning against increasingly sophisticated adaptive threats.
